About & Methodology

How the recommendation algorithm works and the research behind each scoring factor.

Scoring algorithm

FactorWeightLogic
Activity goal50 pts ★
Top priority. Goal tag match = +50, no match = +10. If no goal-matching game exists at the resident's level, all goal-matching games from the nearest level are appended to the results with a level badge and a notice.
Cognitive levelHard filter
Primary pool is always the resident's exact level. Cross-level fallback games only appear when no goal match exists at the primary level, and are clearly labelled.
Time of day15 pts
Evening boosts sensory-tagged games (+3). Morning boosts physical and social (+2 each). Based on sleep research.
Social preference10 pts
Social-enjoying residents get +10 on social games. Struggling residents get −6, nudging towards individual play.
Mobility5 pts
Very limited mobility applies −5 to physical games. Moderate applies −2. Soft penalty only.

Research basis

The time-of-day weighting draws on Richards et al. (2005) and Kuck et al. (2014), who found that physical/social activity during the day and calming stimulation in the evening improve sleep quality in people with dementia.

Han et al. (2016) showed that individualised leisure interventions improve engagement most when tailored to social preferences.

Goal-first priority reflects Deci & Ryan (2000): activities aligned with a specific functional goal produce intrinsically higher engagement than cognitively appropriate but goal-mismatched alternatives.

Important notes

This tool is designed as decision support, not a replacement for professional judgment. Your knowledge of the individual resident always takes precedence.

When a cross-level game is recommended, a level badge and a notice explain why. Always assess whether the suggested level is cognitively appropriate before selecting.

Weights are based on literature review and will be refined once Tovertafel usage log data is analysed.
